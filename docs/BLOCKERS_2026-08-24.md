# Open blockers — what is stopping the engine, and in what order

**Status:** brief, not findings. Everything below was measured on
`data/snapshots/2026-08-13` between 2026-08-21 and 2026-08-24 and is
re-checkable from the repo. Nothing here is a conclusion about what to build.

**Read first:** `analysis/investigations/top100_era_skew.ipynb` (six hypotheses,
four refuted), `docs/DIFFERENTIATOR_AUDIT_2026-08-18.md` (what each mechanism
is worth on held-out prediction), `docs/PLAN_WHOLE_SPORT_ENGINE_2026-08-21.md`
(the design these blockers sit in front of).

## 1. The blocker graph

Arrows mean *blocks*. The chain is the point: **B1 is why the largest measured
improvement in the whole project is sitting unused.**

```
B1  org_weight leaks future information
     └─> B2  cross-org scope quarantined from production
              ├─> B3  scope truncation unfixed  ──> the era skew itself
              └─> B4  whole-career crawl 29% complete
B5  driftless prior deletes peaks ──> Prime/Peak untrustworthy, age work blocked
B6  rank intervals too wide to claim any per-fighter movement
     └─> blocks *validating* any fix to B1–B5 at the fighter level
B7  dense rank over a 2,366-way tie   (independent, cheap)
B8  cross-era scale rests on 14 fighters  (not fixable by modelling)
B9  two silent no-ops make the cross-org flag look like it does nothing
```

## 2. B1 — `org_weight` is computed from the future *(root blocker)*

**What.** `compute_fight_weights` prices a non-UFC bout by both participants'
**UFC-anchored caliber percentiles**. A 2003 PRIDE bout is therefore weighted by
what those two fighters went on to do in the UFC, years later.

**Evidence.** `ratings/rate_snapshot.py`, in the production path:

> Existing cross-org weights were derived from fighters' eventual UFC careers,
> so automatically consuming them here would leak future information into
> historical evidence. They remain an explicitly requested research scope until
> weights are fold-local or outcome-independent.

**Why it blocks.** The leak would corrupt the prequential harness that judges
every other decision, so the scope cannot be promoted and cannot be *evaluated*
honestly either.

**What it is costing.** The audit measured cross-organisation history as worth
**−0.01871 log-loss [−0.02318, −0.01415]** on held-out *UFC* prediction with a
selection control applied — the largest single effect in the engine, larger than
the WHR smoother's own gain over the Glicko filter.

**Resolution — and it is already prototyped.** Plan §E2: a joint fit with **no
organisation weight at all**. That dissolves the leak by construction rather than
patching it — there is no weight derived from future careers because there is no
weight. Promotion strength becomes an *output*, read off the fighters who
crossed. The era-skew notebook already runs this fit (14,619 bouts, 6,296
fighters) and it is the only change that moved the headline: top-100
active-in-2024 **70 → 56**, median debut **2015 → 2011**.

**Do not** instead tune `floor`. The audit found it identified but conflicted:
raising it improves cross-org bout prediction monotonically to 0.75 and makes
UFC bout prediction monotonically *worse*; the current 0.5 is 21st of 35 and
18th of 35. A parameter that is optimal for neither target is evidence for
removing the weight, not for re-tuning it.

**Acceptance test.** Re-run `build_scope_prequential_comparison.py` with the
unweighted joint arm. The 0.0187 gain must survive without the leaked weights,
or the scope change is not justified on prediction and should be argued on
completeness alone — explicitly, not silently.

## 3. B2 — the quarantine, and B9 — the two silent no-ops

**What.** Nothing consumes the cross-org data in production:

1. `rate_snapshot.run(include_experimental_crossorg=False)` and
   `prequential.load_fight_table(with_crossorg=False)` both default off, and
   `refresh.py` never passes either flag.
2. The standard `2026-08-13` snapshot does not contain the file the flag looks
   for. The loader wants a bare `crossorg_fights.parquet`; the snapshot has
   `crossorg_fights.fightmatrix-public.parquet` (suffixed) and
   `fightmatrix_crossorg_fights.parquet`. Only three experimental snapshots
   carry the bare name.

**Why B9 is its own blocker.** Anyone who flips `--experimental-crossorg` on the
standard snapshot merges **zero bouts** and gets an identical board. The obvious
inference — "cross-org makes no difference" — is wrong, and the run gives no
signal that it did nothing. On a UFC-only snapshot every `org_weight` is 1.0, so
the ablation is `0 (exactly), [0, 0]`: inert by construction, not by measurement.

**Resolution.** Make the no-op loud. If `include_experimental_crossorg=True` and
no bare `crossorg_fights.parquet` exists, raise rather than print. A flag that
silently does nothing is worse than a missing flag.

## 4. B3 — scope truncation, and the Unified-Rules boundary *(a decision, not a bug)*

**What.** Two different truncations get conflated. Keep them apart.

**(a) Non-UFC careers are absent.** PRIDE, WEC, Strikeforce, Affliction,
Bellator and RIZIN are ingested (`majors_bouts.parquet`, 6,199 bouts / 589
events) and unused. Measured effect of including them, core-only re-rank:
Wanderlei Silva from the zero-mass tie to 47 on 29 recovered bouts, José Aldo
33 → 11, Mauricio Rua 1,749 → 59, Urijah Faber 2,427 → 140. Blast radius: 24 in,
24 out of the top 100, Spearman 0.940, median top-50 move 14 places.

**(b) UFC 1–27 are excluded on purpose.** This is *not* missing data and *not* a
loader bug — an earlier read of mine said otherwise and was wrong. The 253 bouts
across 30 events (1994-03-11 → 2000-09-22) are scraped, parsed, and written to
`_excluded_bouts.csv` with `exclusion_reason = "pre_unified_rules"`.
`loaders/ufcstats_loader.py` states the rule:

> Unified-rules cutoff. UFC 28 (Tito Ortiz vs Yuki Kondo) was the first event
> under unified rules. Everything strictly before is dropped.

**The uncosted consequence.** That boundary is defensible on its own terms and
nobody costed what it does to the board. It means the engine structurally cannot
rank the 1993–2000 generation, and it is most of why Randy Couture scores zero:
his rated record is 20 bouts from 2000-11-17 against 30 in the FightMatrix
diagnostic cache, so roughly a third of his career — the early third — is
outside the window by design.

**The decision this forces (see §8).** Either the pre-unified era stays out and
the board says so in words — *"ranks the Unified-Rules era; fighters whose
careers predate it are not ranked"* — or the boundary applies to *presentation*
while the bouts still inform the rating. Both are defensible. Silently ranking
Couture 2,032nd is not.

## 5. B4 — the whole-career crawl truncates its own entrants

**What.** The majors ingest is roster-complete *within six promotions*, which
applies the same censoring one level down: a Bellator fighter's non-Bellator
record is missing unless it happens to fall in another major.

**Evidence.** **22 of the joint top 60** hold under half their record inside the
UFC. `crossorg_careers_coverage.json`: the whole-career extension has read
**1,158 of 4,013** fighter pages (29%).

**Why it blocks.** Adopting the joint scope now means inheriting a smaller
version of the problem it fixes. That is a reason to finish the crawl, not a
reason to stay UFC-only.

**Resolution.** `build_crossorg_careers.py --from-cache` for the 2,855 pages
already cached, then crawl the remainder. Bounded: one page per fighter, no new
promotion to curate, no org weight to derive.

## 6. B5 — the driftless prior deletes peaks

**What.** WHR's Wiener prior is driftless: `theta(t+D) − theta(t) ~ N(0, w²D)`.
It asserts a 24-year-old and a 41-year-old are equally likely to improve as to
decline, so the only way to explain "high, then very low" is to lower the whole
trajectory.

**Evidence.** Refitting with one fighter's post-peak suffix deleted, over 412
long careers: **375 (91%)** have that peak revised *down* by the full-career fit,
median **−73 points**, graded **+247 points per unit of post-cut win rate
(t = 18.3)**. **92 of 607** careers with ≥10 bouts are fitted as pure downward
ramps. The sign is wrong for a less-evidence explanation — less evidence pulls a
high rating toward the anchor, not away from it.

**Why it blocks.** Prime and Peak scores cannot be defended while a fighter's
peak is a function of how long they fought afterwards. It also blocks the age
work, which is the same parameter family.

**Resolution.** Plan §E6 / Phase 1b: age-dependent drift in the prior **mean**,
fitted prequentially. **Not** a decline penalty — the gradient already separates
Romero from Ferguson, and a penalty would post the same fact a third time,
breaking Single-Entry.

## 7. B6 — the board is not identified beyond first place

**What.** Bootstrap rank intervals are wide enough that almost no rank
difference is claimable.

**Evidence.** Production 150-replicate bootstrap: median top-50 rank interval
width **157 places**. Jon Jones `[1, 2]` is the only tight interval in the top
20; the next narrowest is 17 wide. Natalia Silva `[19, 195]`, Merab Dvalishvili
`[5, 150]`. When the joint scope was tested, only **1 of 5** checked rank moves
had disjoint intervals.

**Why it blocks.** It blocks *validating* every other fix at the fighter level.
A change can move a point estimate 2,000 places and still be unresolved.

**Resolution.** Two candidate directions, and they are not exclusive:
report mass with its interval rather than rank; or revisit §E5 — the bar at 0.9
rests each career on few terms by design, and that is where the width comes from.
The docstring already states the trade honestly ("the price of measuring the
intended thing"). What is missing is a decision about whether the board should
publish a number it cannot defend.

## 8. B7 — the board prints a dense rank across a 2,366-way tie

**What.** Ranks **189 – 2,554** all hold a career mass of exactly zero — 2,366 of
2,554 fighters, one contiguous block, one distinct value of the secondary sort
key. `career_skill_mass` then breaks the tie on `repr(name)`.

**Consequence.** Forrest Griffin 945, Randy Couture 2,032, Wanderlei Silva 2,476
is **alphabetical ordering presented as a ranking**. Every one of those numbers
reads as a measurement and none of them is.

**Resolution.** Cheap and independent of everything else: rank with
`method="min"` so the tie prints as one place, or publish *"unranked — no year
above the bar"*. Presentation only; no mass changes.

---

## 9. B8 — the cross-era scale rests on 14 fighters

**What.** Comparing a 2000 rating with a 2024 one runs through a chain of
overlapping careers, and the chain has a bottleneck.

**Evidence.** The narrowest year-to-year link is **2000 → 2001, sharing 14
fighters**. Only **10 fighters** in the database were active both in 2000–2004
and in 2016–2026. Against that, conditional on record depth, a later debut is
worth **+5.74 rating points per year (t = 7.73)** — about +138 points across the
span, against a bar that rises only +73.

**Why it blocks.** Whether that gradient is the sport improving or the scale
drifting is **not identified** from this data, and no model choice resolves it.

**Resolution.** There is none by modelling. Publish the bridge width beside any
cross-era claim and treat the gradient as unattributed. The only real remedy is
more record per fighter — which is B3 and B4.

## 12. Constraints that do not change

- **Single-Entry.** Every fact posted once. Opponent quality lives in the
  opponent's rating, achievement in the ledger, era in the reference field. Any
  fix that adds one of them to the score is wrong by construction.
- **No organisation weight.** Promotion strength is an output of the joint fit,
  never an input. A weight asserts the answer the fit exists to estimate.
- **Never tune to an external list.** FightMatrix and the UFC rankings are
  diagnostics to be explained, never targets.
- **Evidence discipline.** "Unresolved" when the interval crosses zero. A rank
  change is not a claim about a fighter without its interval beside it.
- **Re-bootstrap anything that changes the board.** A better point estimate with
  a wider interval has not obviously improved anything, and must say so.
