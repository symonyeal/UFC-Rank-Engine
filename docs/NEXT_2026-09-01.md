## 1. The complaint

The published top 100 is ordered by the all-time score. Every board now also
prints **Prime** (the fighter's level across their best elite decade) and
**Elite wins** (contenders beaten inside it, contender = rated 1750+ at the time
with 8+ UFC bouts of their own). Those two columns frequently disagree with the
rank beside them, in ways a reader notices immediately:

| 44 | Tyron Woodley | 615.5 | 114.0 | 354.5 | 462.3 | 1.00 | 4-3 | 5 | 1803 |
| 47 | Dricus Du Plessis | 592.2 | 631.8 | 387.2 | 268.6 | 0.80 | 3-2 | 6 | 2043 |
| 60 | Michael Chandler | 475.0 | 216.1 | 527.1 | 225.6 | 0.90 | 5-3 | **2** | 1811 |
| 62 | Sean Strickland | 471.6 | 219.7 | 496.8 | 232.0 | 0.90 | 2-0 | **9** | 1918 |
| 75 | Robert Whittaker | 386.9 | 107.5 | 186.5 | 292.8 | 0.90 | 1-0 | 8 | 1900 |

Specific objections raised: Jiri at 100 when his losses are title fights;

**These are symptoms. The question is whether the all-time score is measuring
what it claims to, not whether these nine fighters can be reordered.**

## 2. Already measured — start here, do not redo

All figures below are from snapshot `2026-08-13`, published scope, computed on
`main@6561688`.

### 2.1 What actually drives the rank

Over the published top 100, against the final rank:

| component | share of total score | sd | spearman with rank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| skill | 37.0% | 535.9 | −0.565 |
| title | 35.7% | 509.4 | **−0.721** |
| schedule | 27.3% | 179.1 | **−0.752** |

Schedule is the **weakest** component by mass and the **strongest** by rank
correlation. Skill carries the most mass and predicts rank least. That inversion
is the core finding to explain.

`spearman(score, elite_wins) = 0.488` and `spearman(score, prime level) = 0.421`
across the same 100. The two printed columns explain less than a quarter of the
ordering between them.

### 2.2 The title component is a cliff, not a slope

**14 of the top 100 have a title score of exactly 0.0.** Their median rank is
**86**; the median rank of everyone else is **44.5**. Their median elite-win
count is 4 — they are not weak fighters, they are non-champions.

A component carrying 35.7% of the score and correlating −0.721 with rank is
zero for anyone who never won a belt. Demian Maia (7 elite wins), Beneil Dariush
(6), Anthony Johnson (6) all score 0.0 there. This is the most likely single
explanation for Whittaker, Strickland-vs-Chandler and Sean Brady.

**Do not assume the cliff is wrong.** A résumé score may legitimately reward
championships. The question is whether *a hard zero* is the right shape, and
whether the count that feeds it is comparable across promotions — see 2.3.

### 2.3 Title bouts are 44% non-UFC

Model title bouts by organisation: UFC 439, Bellator 118, UFC pre-unified 35,
ONE 32, WEC 31, RIZIN 25, Strikeforce 21, PRIDE 13. **UFC is 56.2% of them.**

Chandler's 5 title wins are Bellator; Strickland's 2 are UFC. Chandler's title
score (527.1) exceeds Strickland's (496.8) on that count. The ledger prices each
win by the opponent's rating rather than by a flat per-title amount, so this is
not a raw count problem — but opponent ratings inside a thin promotion carry the
inflation described in 2.5, so the pricing inherits it.

### 2.4 The exposure factor

Range across the top 100: 0.54 to 0.97, median 0.86, `spearman(rank) = −0.286`.
It multiplies **both** skill and schedule. Jiri sits at 0.70 and Woodley at
1.00, which is a 30% swing on two of three components before any fight evidence
is considered. It is a hand-typed organisation table
(`ORG_FACTOR_BY_CANONICAL` in `ratings/legacy_resume.py`), not a measured
quantity. Neutralising it was already tried — see §4.

### 2.5 The estimator defect underneath everything

An undefeated record is rated above every opponent it beat, because nothing
bounds the whole-history fit from above. Measured across 20 fighters: those with
~0.8 career losses sit **+79.7** above the best opponent they ever faced;
those with ~4.1 losses sit **−68.1**. Shaidullaev is +302, Khabib +206.

Any component built from opponent ratings inherits this. That includes the title
ledger, the schedule component, and the contender line itself.

### 2.6 Fighters with a strong elite record ranked low

Of the 45 fighters anywhere on the board with ≥6 elite wins, 5 sit outside the
top 100: Glover Teixeira (128), Alexander Volkov (142), Jan Blachowicz (143),
Nassourdine Imavov (241), **Neil Magny (600, 6 elite wins, title 0.0,
schedule 23.4)**. Magny is the extreme case worth explaining in full.

## 3. The work

### 3.1 FIRST — decide what the all-time score is for

The board is titled "what did the fighter's completed career amount to". Two
incompatible readings are currently mixed into one number:

* **Achievement**: belts won and defended. This justifies the title cliff and
  makes Chandler-over-Strickland correct.
* **Quality of work**: who you beat and how good you were. This makes the cliff
  indefensible and Whittaker badly underrated.

**Write the answer down before changing any weight.** If the score is an
achievement measure, the elite columns beside it are decoration and the two will
never agree — in which case say so on the page and stop. If it is a quality
measure, the title component needs a shape that does not zero a non-champion.

### 3.2 Decompose the disagreement properly

For each of the 100 published fighters compute the rank they would hold under
each component alone, and under elite-win mass alone. Report the rank deltas.
The named cases in §1 are the ones to explain, but a fix must be argued on the
whole distribution, not on nine names.

Specifically test whether the schedule component is doing the work the title
component is credited with: it has the highest rank correlation (−0.752) and the
lowest spread, which is the signature of a variable acting as a tiebreak across
the whole board rather than as a measurement.

### 3.3 Test the title cliff directly

Three candidate shapes, all cheap:

1. **Keep the zero.** Argue it from the score's stated purpose.
2. **Floor the component at a title-eliminator/contender credit**, so reaching a
   title fight and losing scores above never contesting one. Jiri's losses being
   title fights is the case this addresses.
3. **Replace the count with the elite-win ledger already computed**, and price
   belts as a multiplier on it rather than as a separate additive term.

Report for each: movement of the nine named fighters, the count of top-100
fighters at exactly 0.0, and agreement with the three external anchor lists
**with the paired bootstrap interval** — and expect the anchors not to resolve
it, per §4.

### 3.4 Decide the promotion question, with a measurement

Establish whether a Bellator title win and a UFC title win should price equally
after the opponent's own rating is accounted for. The pool offset (+54 Elo,
[+10, +100], held out) is the measured quantity in this neighbourhood and is
already used on the title path. Either extend it or state why it does not apply.

## 4. Measured and refuted — do not re-propose

* **Neutralising the exposure factor.** Rank correlation with UFC bout count
  falls +0.483 → +0.345, but zero-UFC fighters in the top 100 double (5 → 9) and
  Patricio Freire returns to 14th. All three arms hold 37 anchors in the top
  100, so the external check does not discriminate.
* **A hinge at the contender bar in the title ledger.** `(2q−1)+` zeroed the
  title component for 38 fighters with 3+ title wins (Shevchenko 11 → 0, Usman
  6 → 0) and put Matt Serra 50th all-time on a 7-7 record.
* **Splitting the quality ledger on title status.** Five aggregations tried;
  every one raised Freire and destroyed the external fit.
* **Swapping the title ledger to causal Glicko ratings.** Hughes 427.4 → 444.7,
  Spearman 0.9149 across 245 fighters. Replaces the timing problem with a
  prior-dominated one.
* **Unifying the title bar onto the division contender line.** Made the board
  worse: St-Pierre's title résumé fell to 279 against Namajunas's 657, and
  Namajunas reached 9th all-time. A higher bar reads the 2.5 estimator error
  *more* sensitively, not less.
* **Raising `WHR_VIRTUAL_GAMES`.** Refuted twice; the predictive fit moved it
  down, 2.0 → 1.0.
* **The anchor lists as an acceptance test for a small change.** Ten to
  thirty-four hand-picked names. The paired bootstrap does not resolve a 54-Elo
  ledger correction. Use them to catch a *large* reordering, never to select a
  parameter.
* **Ranking the elite board by the bare Prime level** (Topuria 5 wins above
  St-Pierre 11), **by opponent strength with results ignored** (Roy Nelson 0-10
  above Khabib and St-Pierre), or **by evidence-discounted level** (saturates;
  Silva and Nemkov 1.2 points apart at k=20). Settled: elite-win mass.

# OUTCOME — closed 2026-09-01

## §2.1 of this brief was wrong

Its three shares (37.0 / 35.7 / 27.3) are the components' **raw** sums, taken
before each is divided by its own maximum. On the scale that actually enters the
score: skill **21.9%**, title **25.1%**, schedule **53.0%**. There was no
inversion. Schedule was the largest by mass *and* the strongest by rank
correlation, and the board changed its own definition at about rank 25 —
variance share 36.6 / 38.9 / 24.5 in ranks 1–25 against 12.2 / 29.1 / **58.7**
in ranks 26–100.

Cause: `_unit` divided by a maximum, a single order statistic, meeting three
different tail shapes (max/median 10.6 skill, 8.3 title, **2.9** schedule). 52%
of the top 100 sat below a tenth of skill's max and 43% below a tenth of title's,
against 3% for schedule. The file's claim that this combined the three "without
an exchange rate anyone had to invent" was false: the rate was set by whichever
career topped each column.

## The schedule component's bar was undocumented and non-comparable

`RANK_CONTEXT_TOP_SHARE = 0.20` was adopted 2026-08-26 against a table of median
active division fields of 37–85. On the published scope those fields are
**144–1435**, so `0.20 × pool > 15` on **92.7%** of appearances and the share
rule changed the window on 7.0% — almost all catchweight rows. Top fifteen is the
top 1.05% of lightweight against 2.88% of light heavyweight. Only 3.93% of wins
scored any credit; 42.9% of wins over a contender scored zero, and 77.8% of
credited wins were below the line. It correlated **+0.360 with elite losses** and
**+0.013 with elite W−L** — gatekeeping, the failure this project already
rejected for the elite board, inside the majority of the published score's mass.

## Shipped

`_unit` → `_scale` (mean of the column's own top 100). `contender_resume_ledger`
replaces the rank-context component, screened on the board's own contender line
(1750 + 8 UFC bouts) and priced by `title_quality` against it, opponent rating
read pre-fight, capped at one contribution per active year. Stated weights:
`LEGACY_ACHIEVEMENT_WEIGHT = 0.30`, `LEGACY_QUALITY_SKILL_SHARE = 0.25`, both set
by the project owner, both swept in the code beside the constant.

Acceptance, correlations over a fixed population: ρ(score, elite wins)
0.519 → **0.730**, ρ(score, prime) 0.418 → **0.632**, explicit non-anchors in the
top 100 6 → **2**, external-only 4 → **2**, `WATCH_BELOW_40` names outside the top
40 two → **none**. The 100 Greatest +0.418 → +0.580 (Δρ +0.162, CI95
[−0.009, +0.364]); every other anchor interval straddles zero. §4 of this brief
was right — the anchors selected none of it, and none of it was selected on them.

## §3.4 answered: no further promotion term

After the +54 offset, a UFC title win prices at mean weight 0.119 against
Bellator's 0.059 (bootstrap difference +0.061, CI95 [+0.044, +0.078]). The
discount is present and working through the opponent's rating.

## Carried-forward item 1: both title shapes re-measured, both REJECTED

Measured on the current two-term score over the fixed incumbent top 100; the
harness reproduces the live score and the live title-win ledger to 0.0 first.

* **Credit a title-fight loss** (c = 0.25 / 0.50) drops the cliff 15 -> 7 / 6 and
  moves Prochazka 139 -> 129 / 121, but raises **Sean Strickland 20 -> 19 -> 17**.
  Strickland is this project's named symptom of scoring exposure rather than
  contention, and the component retired earlier the same day correlated +0.360
  with elite losses. Paying for a title-fight loss is that component under the
  achievement term's name. rho(elite wins) +0.013/+0.018, rho(prime)
  **-0.009/-0.019**.
* **Elite-win mass with belts as a multiplier** shows the largest numbers in the
  table (rho elite wins 0.730 -> 0.816, rho prime 0.632 -> 0.767) and they are
  **circular**: the variant's title term IS `elite_wins * (elite_level - anchor)`,
  and those two statistics are rho(score, elite_wins) and rho(score,
  elite_level). Every external anchor is unmoved. It also breaks the term --
  Matt Serra, a UFC champion, scores **0.0** while Neil Magny, who never fought
  for a title, scores 50.2 -- and double-posts the resume ledger.

All sixteen anchor intervals straddle zero. The 100 Greatest is the only list
with power (resolution sd 0.003 against 0.063-0.065) and both shapes are flat to
slightly negative on it.

**The cliff stays open and unchanged at 15.** Note for the next pass: 0.0 for a
fighter who never won a title is what a championship term *should* say. The case
that is genuinely wrong is item 2 -- a former UFC champion pricing at 0.008 and
0.054 because his title opponents sat below their division-year bar. That is
the title-quality pricing, not the cliff.
