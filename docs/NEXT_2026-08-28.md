## 2. What is already settled, and must not be re-derived

The operating contract is `New Work Folder (Former Codex)\CLAUDE.md`. Three rules
bind this work:

- **Count each fact once.** Every fact about a fight is recorded exactly once.
  Career length already has a home in career skill mass, which takes **at most
  one contribution per active calendar year**. Anything else that grows with
  career length is recording it a second time.
- **How a change gets accepted.** A change to the ratings has to pass a held-out
  prediction test. A score that never predicts a fight cannot be judged that way,
  so a score change is argued from mechanism and checked against outside

## 3. The three mechanisms, measured

### 3.1 Hughes — the model has no opinion about *when* a fighter was good

The rating is fitted across a fighter's whole career at once. For an elite career
with few losses there is almost no evidence about which years were the good ones,
so the fitted line is nearly flat and the fighter's peak level is carried all the
way back to their debut.

Measured on Georges St-Pierre, 28 appearances:

| | value |
|---|---:|
| first appearance, 2002-01-25 | **2065.8** |
| at the Hughes fight, 2004-10-22 | **2056.2** |
| career maximum | 2090.4 |
| **total spread across 28 appearances** | **34.2 Elo** |

So the model believes a 20-year-old St-Pierre on debut was already within 25
points of peak St-Pierre. When Hughes beat him in October 2004, the title résumé
priced that opponent at **2058.8**.

Hughes's nine title-win opponents, as priced:

| date | opponent | priced before the fight |
|---|---|---:|
| 2001-11-02 | Carlos Newton | 1585.5 |
| 2002-03-22 | Hayato Sakurai | 1725.7 |
| 2002-07-13 | Carlos Newton | 1568.4 |
| 2002-11-22 | Gil Castillo | 1609.3 |
| 2003-04-25 | Sean Sherk | 1904.5 |
| 2003-11-21 | Frank Trigg | 1765.9 |
| **2004-10-22** | **Georges St-Pierre** | **2058.8** |
| 2005-04-16 | Frank Trigg | 1736.6 |
| 2006-09-23 | BJ Penn | 1838.2 |

The résumé raises each win's quality to the **fourth power**, so a 200-point gap
over the next-best opponent becomes a very large multiple. Only **3 of Hughes's
9 title wins clear their division's contender bar**, and yet his title component
is 1149.5.

**Note the counter-example in the same table.** BJ Penn is priced at 1838.2
before the fight against a career average of 1451.5 — a 387-point gap the *other*
way. The fitted line is not uniformly flat; it is flat for careers with few
losses. Any fix has to handle both.

### 3.2 Henderson — the schedule component counts wins, it does not price them

The schedule score is the number of credited wins multiplied by an exposure
factor, where each credited win contributes at most 0.08.

Henderson's credited wins:

| | before the duplicate fix | now |
|---|---:|---:|
| appearances | 70 | **47** |
| **credited wins** | 28 | **20** |
| average credit | 1.063 | 1.062 |
| **observed credit range** | 1.064 – 1.080 | **1.021 – 1.080** |
| total | 1.760 | **1.248** |

Beating Anderson Silva scores **1.080**. The median credited win scores about
**1.062**. Most of the weight sits in the top sixth of an 8% band, so in practice
the component is close to a **count** of ranked wins rather than a pricing of
them. Across the top 150 it still correlates **+0.779** with credited-win count,
against **+0.191** with total appearances.

The duplicate fix removed the crudest version of the problem — Renato Sobral has
gone from 40th on an inflated 102 appearances to **141st** on a true 49 — but the
component still has no per-year cap, so a long career with many ranked wins keeps
accumulating without limit. Career skill mass caps at one contribution per active
year and this does not, which means career length is recorded twice.

### 3.3 The promotion factor — typed in by hand, load-bearing, unjustified

`ORG_FACTOR_BY_CANONICAL` in `ratings/legacy_resume.py` is a hand-typed table
(UFC 1.0, PRIDE 0.95, Strikeforce 0.88, Bellator 0.65, PFL/RIZIN/ONE 0.60 …). It
was **removed from the title path** on 2026-08-25 and **remains on the exposure
path**, where it multiplies both the skill and schedule components.

After the 2026-08-28 weight class fill, it is the dominant remaining source of
promotion bias in the published score:

| quantity | correlation with UFC fight count, top 150 |
|---|---:|
| raw ranked-win figure | +0.327 |
| **exposure factor** | **+0.699** |
| the two multiplied (the schedule score) | +0.483 |

Average exposure factor by UFC experience: **0.560** for fighters with no UFC
fights, 0.717 (1–7), 0.822 (8–14), **0.877** (15 or more). Fedor 0.768, Miocic
0.892.

**Removing it was already tested and rejected** — see §5.

## 4. The work, in order

### 4.1 FIRST — decide what a title win's opponent value *means*

This comes first because it is a definitional question, and both §3.1 and the
existing fourth-power setting depend on the answer.

The résumé currently asks: *how good was this opponent, using everything we now
know?* That is a defensible backward-looking question, and it is why excluding
the fight itself is not enough of a guard — it does not exclude the next fifteen
years.

The alternative: *how good did this opponent look at the time?* That is what a
reader means by "Hughes beat a young GSP".

**Pick one, in writing, and say which question the published board answers.** Then
implement it. Do not ship a blend without saying what the blend measures.

What is already known:

- **Swapping in fight-by-fight ratings does not fix it**, and this was measured:
  Hughes went 427.4 → **444.7** title points, slightly *up*. Agreement across 245
  fighters was 0.9149. Do not simply re-run that.
- **Why it failed is worth understanding before trying again.** A fight-by-fight
  rating at a fighter's third bout is dominated by the starting assumption, so a
  young GSP becomes *average* rather than *unproven but good*. What you actually
  want is the estimate at that date **given only fights up to that date**, with
  its uncertainty carried — not a single number from either extreme.
- **Worth testing:** price the win by the opponent's rating before the fight
  **and** its uncertainty, discounting a win over a fighter the model had not yet
  pinned down. `run_whr(return_variance=True)` exposes a variance already; read
  its documentation first, because it is explicitly not the quantity you might
  assume.
- **Cheaper, and worth pricing before the expensive option:** cap what any single
  win can contribute, or replace the sum with an average of the best few, so no
  one win can be half a career. Measure what that does to Hughes, St-Pierre,
  Jones and Silva.

**Acceptance:** this is a score change, so prediction error cannot judge it. Argue
the mechanism, then report agreement with all three outside lists **with the
confidence interval**, and expect it not to resolve — see §5.

### 4.2 SECOND — give the schedule component a shape

Two separate problems, fixable independently:

**(a) It counts instead of pricing.** The credited range is 1.064–1.080 on an
0.08 band. Either widen the gradient so beating a champion is worth materially
more than beating the 20th-ranked fighter, or price each win the same way the
title résumé does. The second is the coherent choice — one rule for "who did you
beat", used twice — but check it against the count-once rule first: the title
résumé already prices title wins, so a unified rule must not price the same win
in both components.

**(b) It has no per-year cap.** Career skill mass takes at most one contribution
per active year and this does not. Test a per-year cap, and test keeping only the
best few wins of a career. Watch Renato Sobral at 40th on 102 appearances and
Henderson's 28 credited wins.

**Acceptance:** report the board movement for Henderson, Hughes, Couture, Sobral,
Jones and St-Pierre, and the repaired component's correlation with credited-win
count (currently **+0.779**) and with appearances (**+0.191**).

### 4.3 THIRD — replace the typed promotion factor with a measured one, or delete it

Only after 4.1 and 4.2, because both change what the exposure factor multiplies.

The table is unjustified as a hand-typed constant. The honest options:

1. **Measure it.** The 2026-08-28 pass measured a held-out gap of **+54 Elo
   [+10, +100]** for UFC-tested fighters against never-UFC ones, and used it on
   the title path. That is a correction on the *rating* scale, not a value
   multiplier, so it is not a drop-in replacement — but it is the measured
   quantity in the neighbourhood. Deriving a per-promotion factor from held-out
   crossover fights, which `build_org_strength_audit.py` was built to do, is the
   principled path.
2. **Delete it and accept the consequence.** Measured: the correlation falls
   +0.483 → +0.345, but fighters with no UFC record in the top 100 double (5 → 9)
   and Patricio Freire returns to 14th.
3. **Keep it and say so.** This is the current state, recorded in
   [How the ratings and the score are built](RATING_LAYER_AND_LEDGER_2026-08-28.md)
   §5b.

**Do not** simply retune the numbers by hand to make a board look better. If the
factor survives, it needs a measurement behind each value, or a label saying
plainly that it is an assumption.

## 5. Tested and rejected — do not propose these again

- **Swapping the title résumé to fight-by-fight ratings.** Hughes 427.4 → 444.7,
  agreement 0.9149 across 245 fighters, median movement 20.7 points. It does not
  fix the timing problem; it replaces it with a different one.
- **Removing the exposure factor.** Correlation +0.483 → +0.345, but fighters
  with no UFC record in the top 100 go 5 → 9 and Freire returns to 14th, undoing
  the promotion-corrected title work. All three versions hold **37 listed
  fighters in the top 100**, so the outside check cannot tell them apart.
- **A hard cut-off at the contender bar in the title résumé.** It zeroed the
  title component for 38 fighters with three or more title wins (Shevchenko
  11 → 0, Usman 6 → 0) while putting Matt Serra 50th all-time on a 7–7 record.
- **Splitting the quality résumé on title status** (title wins in one, the rest
  in another). Five versions tried; every one raised Freire and wrecked agreement
  with the outside lists. A dominant champion fights his best opponents for the
  belt, so the split rewards perennial contenders instead.
- **Raising `WHR_VIRTUAL_GAMES`.** Rejected twice, most recently on the repaired
  data: the predictive fit moved it **down**, 2.0 → 1.0.
- **Using the outside lists to judge a small change.** Ten to thirty-four
  hand-picked names cannot resolve a 54-Elo correction, and they could not
  separate the three exposure versions. Use them to catch a *large* reordering,
  never to choose a setting.
