## 2. What is already established, and must not be re-derived

## 3. The three mechanisms, measured

### 3.1 Hughes — the smoother has no opinion about *when*

`mu_whr` is a **whole-history smoother**. For a low-loss elite career it has
almost no evidence about which years were the good ones, so the fitted
trajectory is nearly flat and the fighter's peak level is backfilled to their
debut.

Measured on Georges St-Pierre, 28 appearances:

| | value |
|---|---:|
| first appearance, 2002-01-25 | **2065.8** |
| at the Hughes fight, 2004-10-22 | **2056.2** |
| career maximum | 2090.4 |
| **total spread across 28 appearances** | **34.2 Elo** |

So the model believes a 20-year-old St-Pierre on debut was already within 25
points of peak St-Pierre. When Hughes beat him in October 2004, the title ledger
priced that opponent at **2058.8**.

Hughes's nine title-win opponents as priced:

| date | opponent | priced pre-fight |
|---|---|---:|
| 2001-11-02 | Carlos Newton | 1585.5 |
| 2002-03-22 | Hayato Sakurai | 1725.7 |
| 2002-07-13 | Carlos Newton | 1568.4 |
| 2002-11-22 | Gil Castillo | 1609.3 |
| 2003-04-25 | Sean Sherk | 1904.5 |
| 2003-11-21 | Frank Trigg | 1765.9 |
| **2004-10-22** | **Georges St-Pierre** | **2058.8** |
| 2005-04-16 | Frank Trigg | 1736.6 |
| 2006-09-23 | BJ Penn | 1838.2 |

The ledger weight is `q ** TITLE_QUALITY_EXPONENT` with the exponent at **4**, so
a 200-point gap over the next-best opponent is a very large multiple. Only
**3 of Hughes's 9 title wins clear their division's contender line**, and yet
his title component is 1149.5.

**Note the counter-example in the same table.** BJ Penn is priced at 1838.2
pre-fight against a career `mu_whr` of 1451.5 — a 387-point gap the *other* way.
The smoother is not uniformly flat; it is flat for careers with few losses. Any
fix has to handle both.

### 3.2 Henderson — the schedule component counts, it does not price

`public_legacy_schedule_score = RANK_CONTEXT_WIN_POINTS * rank_context_win_mass
* exposure_factor`, where `rank_context_win_mass` is the **sum over wins** of
`perf_factor_rank_context - 1`.

`PERF_RANK_CONTEXT_AMPLITUDE = 0.08`, so each credited win is worth at most
0.08. Henderson's 28 credited wins:

| | before the fan-out fix | now |
|---|---:|---:|
| appearances | 70 | **47** |
| **credited wins** | 28 | **20** |
| mean credit | 1.063 | 1.062 |
| **observed credit range** | 1.064 – 1.080 | **1.021 – 1.080** |
| summed mass | 1.760 | **1.248** |

Beating Anderson Silva scores **1.080**. The median credited win scores about
**1.062**. That is most of the mass sitting in the top sixth of an 8% band, so in
practice the component is close to a **count of ranked wins** rather than a
pricing of them. Across the published top 150 it still correlates **+0.779**
with credited-win count, against **+0.191** with raw appearances.

The fan-out fix removed the crudest version of the problem — Renato Sobral has
gone from 40th on a bloated 102 appearances to **141st** on a true 49 — but the
component still has no per-year cap, so a long career with many ranked wins
still accumulates without bound. Career Skill Mass caps at one contribution per
active year and this does not, which is the Single-Entry violation: career length
is posted twice, and the irredundancy matrix assigns activity/layoff to
"display-time attenuation, clearly labelled; **not** career mass".

**This is a Single-Entry violation.** Career Skill Mass deliberately caps at one
contribution per active year. The schedule component has no such cap, so career
length is posted twice — and the irredundancy matrix explicitly assigns
activity/layoff to "display-time attenuation, clearly labelled; **not** career
mass".

### 3.3 The organisation factor — typed, load-bearing, and unjustified

`ORG_FACTOR_BY_CANONICAL` in `ratings/legacy_resume.py` is a hand-typed table
(UFC 1.0, PRIDE 0.95, Strikeforce 0.88, Bellator 0.65, PFL/RIZIN/ONE 0.60 …). It
was **removed from the title path** on 2026-08-25 and **remains on the exposure
path**, where it multiplies both the skill and schedule components.

After the 2026-08-28 schedule division fill, it is the dominant remaining source
of promotion bias in the published score:

| quantity | spearman with UFC bout count, top 150 |
|---|---:|
| raw rank-context win mass | +0.327 |
| **exposure factor** | **+0.699** |
| their product (= schedule score) | +0.483 |

Mean exposure factor by UFC exposure: **0.560** for fighters with no UFC bouts,
0.717 (1–7), 0.822 (8–14), **0.877** (15+). Fedor 0.768, Miocic 0.892.

**Neutralising it was already measured and rejected** — see §5.

## 4. The work, in order

### 4.1 FIRST — decide what a title win's opponent value *means*

This is first because it is a definitional question, and both 3.1 and the
existing `TITLE_QUALITY_EXPONENT = 4` sweep depend on the answer.

The ledger currently asks: *how good was this opponent, using everything we now
know?* That is a defensible retrospective question, and it is why
`merge_asof(allow_exact_matches=False)` is not a sufficient guard — excluding
the bout itself does not exclude the next fifteen years.

The alternative: *how good did this opponent look at the time?* That is the
causal question, and it is what a reader means by "Hughes beat a young GSP".

**Pick one, in writing, and say which question the published board answers.**
Then implement it. Do not ship a blend without saying what the blend estimates.

What is already known:
* A **blind source swap to causal Glicko does not fix it** and was measured:
  Hughes went 427.4 → **444.7** title display points, i.e. slightly *up*. Across
  245 fighters Spearman was 0.9149. Do not simply re-run that.
* The reason the swap failed is worth understanding before trying again: causal
  Glicko at a fighter's third bout is dominated by the prior, so a young GSP
  becomes *average*, not *unproven-but-good*. The quantity you want is closer to
  "the posterior at that date **given only bouts up to that date**" with its
  uncertainty carried, not a point estimate from either extreme.
* **A candidate worth testing:** price the win by the opponent's *pre-fight*
  rating **and** its uncertainty, discounting a win over a fighter the model had
  not yet resolved. `run_whr(return_variance=True)` already exposes a
  conditional block-curvature variance; read its docstring first, because it is
  explicitly *not* a marginal posterior variance.
* **A cheaper candidate, worth pricing before the expensive one:** cap the
  per-win contribution, or replace the sum with a top-k mean, so no single win
  can be half a career. Measure what that does to Hughes, GSP, Jones and Silva.

**Acceptance:** this is a ledger change, so log loss cannot validate it. Argue
the mechanism, then report agreement with all three external anchor lists **with
the paired bootstrap interval**, and expect it not to resolve — see §5.

### 4.2 SECOND — give the schedule component a shape

Two separate defects, and they can be fixed independently:

**(a) It counts instead of pricing.** The credited range is 1.064–1.080 on an
0.08 amplitude. Either widen the gradient so beating a champion is worth
materially more than beating the 20th-ranked fighter, or replace
`perf_factor_rank_context` on this path with the same per-opponent pricing the
title ledger uses (`title_quality`, `q ** exponent`, against the opponent's own
division-year line). The second is the coherent choice — one pricing rule for
"who did you beat", used twice — but check it against Single-Entry first: the
title ledger already prices title wins, so a unified rule must not price the
same win in both components.

**(b) It has no per-year cap.** Career Skill Mass takes at most one contribution
per active calendar year, and this does not. Test a per-year cap, and test a
top-k over the career. The target to watch is Renato Sobral at 40th on 102
appearances, and Henderson's 28 credited wins.

**Acceptance:** report the board movement for Henderson, Hughes, Couture,
Sobral, Jones and St-Pierre, and the correlation of the repaired component with
credited-win count (currently **+0.779**) and with appearances (**+0.191**).

### 4.3 THIRD — replace the typed organisation factor with a measured one, or delete it

Only after 4.1 and 4.2, because both change what the exposure factor multiplies.

The table is unjustified as a typed constant. The honest options:

1. **Derive it.** The 2026-08-28 pass measured a held-out pool offset of
   **+54 Elo [+10, +100]** for UFC-tested fighters against never-UFC ones, and
   used it on the title path. That is a *rating-scale* correction, not a value
   multiplier, so it is not a drop-in — but it is the measured quantity in the
   neighbourhood. Deriving a per-promotion factor from held-out crossing bouts,
   the way `build_org_strength_audit.py` was built to do, is the principled path.
2. **Delete it and accept the consequence.** Measured: correlation falls
   +0.483 → +0.345, but zero-UFC fighters in the top 100 double (5 → 9) and
   Patricio Freire returns to 14th.
3. **Keep it and say so.** This is the current state, and it is recorded in
   `docs/RATING_LAYER_AND_LEDGER_2026-08-28.md` §5b.

**Do not** simply retune the numbers by hand to make a board look better. If the
factor survives, it needs a measurement behind each value or an explicit label
saying it is a prior.

## 5. Measured and refuted — do not re-propose

* **Swapping the title ledger to causal Glicko ratings.** Hughes 427.4 → 444.7,
  Spearman 0.9149 across 245 fighters, median absolute movement 20.7 points.
  It does not fix the timing problem; it replaces it with a prior-dominated one.
* **Neutralising the exposure factor.** Correlation +0.483 → +0.345, but
  zero-UFC fighters in the top 100 go 5 → 9 and Freire returns to 14th, undoing
  the pool-priced title work. All three arms hold **37 anchors in the top 100**,
  so the external check does not discriminate between them.
* **A hinge at the contender bar in the title ledger.** `(2q-1)+` zeroed the
  title component for 38 fighters with 3+ title wins (Shevchenko 11 → 0, Usman
  6 → 0) while putting Matt Serra 50th all-time on a 7-7 record.
* **Splitting the quality ledger on title status** (title wins in one ledger,
  the rest in another). Five aggregations tried; every one raised Freire and
  destroyed the external fit. A dominant champion fights his best opponents for
  the belt, so the partition rewards perennial contenders.
* **Raising `WHR_VIRTUAL_GAMES`.** Refuted twice, most recently on the repaired
  corpus: the predictive fit moved it **down**, 2.0 → 1.0.
* **The anchor lists as an acceptance test for a small change.** Ten to
  thirty-four hand-picked names; the paired bootstrap does not resolve a
  54-Elo ledger correction, and it did not discriminate the three exposure arms.
  Use them to catch a *large* reordering, never to select a parameter.
