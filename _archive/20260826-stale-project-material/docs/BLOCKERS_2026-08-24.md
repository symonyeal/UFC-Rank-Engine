**Status:** brief, not findings. Everything below was measured on
`data/snapshots/2026-08-13` between 2026-08-21 and 2026-08-24 and is
re-checkable from the repo. Nothing here is a conclusion about what to build.

**Read first:** `analysis/investigations/top100_era_skew.ipynb` (six hypotheses,

B5, B8 and B10 were addressed after this register was written. Production now
uses the `majors,pre_unified` scope, an age-aware two-stage WHR prior, and the

### The correction that matters most

This register treats "cross-org" as one scope. There are **two corpora, and they
move the board in opposite directions**:

| scope | bouts | top-100 active in 2024 | median debut | pre-2010 debuts in top 100 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `ufc` | 8,479 | 70 | 2015 | 18 |
| `majors` — Sherdog, six promotions, crawled by event | 14,617 | **56** | 2013 | 32 |
| `fightmatrix` — bounded ranked-cohort crawl | 18,312 | **85** | 2012 | 28 |
| `majors,pre_unified` | 14,869 | **55** | 2012 | 33 |

Section 2's "70 → 56" and section 4's Wanderlei/Rua/Faber recoveries are the
**majors** corpus. Section 3's silent no-op, section 5's coverage figure and the
−0.0187 predictive gain are the **FightMatrix** corpus. They were never the same
thing, so the `B1 → B2 → B3(a)` chain in section 1 does not hold as drawn.

### And they are justified on different grounds

Both were put through the same held-out test, paired on identical UFC bouts,
with the selection control applied — the `both fighters covered` band, which
holds coverage constant and leaves only the *content* of the extra history to
explain a difference:

| scope | both-covered log-loss delta | n | verdict |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `fightmatrix` | **−0.01896 [−0.02376, −0.01397]** | 2,117 | helps, and survives removing the leaked weight |
| `majors` | **+0.00391 [−0.00484, +0.01245]** | 435 | **unresolved**, and the point estimate is the wrong sign |

That answers question 3 of section 11 for the corpus it actually applies to.
**`majors` is justified on completeness, explicitly, and not on prediction.**
The reason is structural rather than a defect: `majors` back-fills 1997-2012
history for fighters who are mostly retired before the 2010+ evaluation window
opens, which is why only 435 held-out bouts have both fighters covered at all.
A corpus whose purpose is ranking history should not be expected to forecast
next Saturday's card, and claiming it does would be the same category error as
tuning to FightMatrix.

The reason they differ is in how each was built. The majors crawl walks six
promotions **by event**, so it is roster-complete inside them and reaches back to
1997. The FightMatrix crawl is seeded from **today's ranked fighters**, so it
back-fills the modern regional circuit and cannot reach a fighter who retired
before the rankings it was seeded from. Neither is wrong; they answer different
questions, and merging them under one flag guaranteed a result nobody could
explain.

### One bug found that is not in this register

The cross-org merge admitted 81 UFC-labelled bouts into a table defined as
cross-organisation. Nineteen of those were the *same bout* as a canonical row —
same pair, same date, different source URL — so admitting the scope updated
those ratings twice, once at weight 1.0 and once at an org weight. The tables
also carried bouts recorded twice with **opposite winners**.

Cause: two producers once wrote the same generic artifact name and only one
filtered. `loaders/fightmatrix_profiles` kept `org != "UFC"` and removed bouts
already in `canonical_fights`; the older `analysis/fightmatrix_graph` path
computed `is_cross_organization` without applying it.
`ratings/scope.py::scope_guard` now drops all three classes at the merge point,
the FightMatrix writer filters at source, and source-specific artifact names
keep the producers from drifting into the same file.

## 0b. B10 — the year bar is calibrated to a population that no longer holds

`DEFAULT_CAREER_REFERENCE = 0.9` is justified in `ratings/symon_score.py` by a
**count**: roughly 60 fighter-years out of roughly 578 in a modern year, "a
dozen divisions' top five, the contender line". A quantile only names that count
while the population stays that size, and it no longer does. Measured on
`2026-08-13`:

| scope | year | rated fighter-years | clear the 0.9 bar |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `ufc` | 2005 | 100 | **10** |
| `ufc` | 2015 | 570 | 57 |
| `ufc` | 2024 | 625 | 63 |
| `majors,pre_unified` | 2005 | 1,961 | 197 |
| `majors,pre_unified` | 2015 | 4,190 | 419 |
| `majors,pre_unified` | 2024 | 2,441 | 245 |

Two separate drifts, and they point opposite ways:

1. **Across scopes.** Admitting a second corpus multiplies the rated population
   without changing the sport, and the same bar then admits four to seven times
   as many fighters. A regional-circuit journeyman clears a line chosen to mean
   "a dozen divisions' top five".
2. **Across eras, inside one scope.** On UFC-only the 0.9 bar admits **10**
   fighter-years in 2005 and **63** in 2024. As a *count* it is six times more
   permissive now than it was then — which is a per-era handicap nobody chose.

This is **not** a re-opening of the settled item in section 0. That one refuted
a claim about the bar's *composition* — none of the seven zero-mass fighters
reached the top decile of any year they fought in, which is still true. This is
about its *calibration*, which is a different quantity and was never tested.

### Why it is urgent rather than tidy

The bar moves the era headline **at least as much as the scope does**:

| scope | bar | ranked places | top-100 active 2024 | median debut | pre-2010 debuts |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `ufc` | 0.9 | 189 | 70 | 2015 | 18 |
| `ufc` | `count:60` | 398 | **45** | 2009 | 50 |
| `majors,pre_unified` | 0.9 | 1,273 | 57 | 2009 | 57 |
| `majors,pre_unified` | `count:60` | 469 | **36** | **2003** | **71** |

Changing only the bar, on the unchanged UFC-only scope, moves top-100
active-in-2024 from 70 to 45. The whole era-skew investigation attributed that
headline to scope. Scope is part of it; the bar is the other part, and the two
were never separated.

### What was built, and what was deliberately not

`year_reference` now accepts `"count:<n>"` — the level the n-th best
fighter-year of that year reached, which states the intent directly and is
invariant to how many fighters were rated. A year thinner than `n` falls back to
its own minimum.

**At the time of this register, the default was unchanged at 0.9.** Neither bar is obviously right and the
choice is not the kind of thing to slip into a cleanup:

* a **quantile** holds a constant *fraction*, which is right if the sport's
  shape is stable and only the roster grew;
* a **count** holds a constant *number*, which is right if "contender" is an
  absolute quantity — but it makes early years much easier, because the early
  UFC genuinely was smaller. `count:60` in UFC-2005 falls back to the 60th of
  100 fighter-years, around the 40th percentile, which is nowhere near a
  contender line either.

Neither is defensible without deciding what the board is asserting about a 2005
fighter-year versus a 2024 one. That is a judgement, and it belongs with whoever
owns the board.

## 2. B1 — `org_weight` is computed from the future *(RESOLVED; the premise was partly wrong — see 0a)*

**What.** `compute_fight_weights` prices a non-UFC bout by both participants'
**UFC-anchored caliber percentiles**. A 2003 PRIDE bout is therefore weighted by
what those two fighters went on to do in the UFC, years later.

**Evidence.** `ratings/rate_snapshot.py`, in the production path:

> Existing cross-org weights were derived from fighters' eventual UFC careers,
> so automatically consuming them here would leak future information into
> historical evidence. They remain an explicitly requested research scope until
> weights are fold-local or outcome-independent.

**Why it blocks.** The leak would corrupt the prequential harness that judges
every other decision, so the scope cannot be promoted and cannot be *evaluated*
honestly either.

**What it is costing.** The audit measured cross-organisation history as worth
**−0.01871 log-loss [−0.02318, −0.01415]** on held-out *UFC* prediction with a
selection control applied — the largest single effect in the engine, larger than
the WHR smoother's own gain over the Glicko filter.

**Resolution — and it is already prototyped.** Plan §E2: a joint fit with **no
organisation weight at all**. That dissolves the leak by construction rather than
patching it — there is no weight derived from future careers because there is no
weight. Promotion strength becomes an *output*, read off the fighters who
crossed. The era-skew notebook already runs this fit (14,619 bouts, 6,296
fighters) and it is the only change that moved the headline: top-100
active-in-2024 **70 → 56**, median debut **2015 → 2011**.

> **Correction, 2026-08-24.** Two things in this section are wrong.
>
> 1. *"The leak would corrupt the prequential harness that judges every other
>    decision."* It did not. `_run_canonical_engine` consumes
>    `fighter_a, fighter_b, winner, is_draw, method_score_winner` and no weight
>    column, so the `canonical` and `method` variants — the two the Phase E
>    table was built from — never read `org_weight` at all. Re-running the
>    comparison with a third, unweighted joint arm gives `max_abs_p_gap =
>    0.00000` for both: the weighted and unweighted arms are the *same model*.
>    Only `method_integrity_performance` reads the weight, and there the gain
>    survives its removal (−0.01994 → −0.01925, intervals almost coincident).
>    **The acceptance test passes.**
> 2. The 14,619-bout fit is the **majors** corpus, not the corpus behind
>    `--experimental-crossorg`. The FightMatrix joint fit is 18,312 bouts and
>    moves the headline the other way, to 85 active in 2024.
>
> What *was* genuinely leaking is the production path: `run_whr` reads
> `org_weight` through the bout weights. That is now closed —
> `rate_snapshot.run` discards any staged `org_weight` unless
> `--experimental-org-weight` is passed, and `Variant.use_org_weight` defaults
> to `False` so a research arm cannot consume it by accident.


**Do not** instead tune `floor`. The audit found it identified but conflicted:
raising it improves cross-org bout prediction monotonically to 0.75 and makes
UFC bout prediction monotonically *worse*; the current 0.5 is 21st of 35 and
18th of 35. A parameter that is optimal for neither target is evidence for
removing the weight, not for re-tuning it.

**Acceptance test.** Re-run `build_scope_prequential_comparison.py` with the
unweighted joint arm. The 0.0187 gain must survive without the leaked weights,
or the scope change is not justified on prediction and should be argued on
completeness alone — explicitly, not silently.

## 3. B2 — the quarantine, and B9 — the two silent no-ops *(RESOLVED)*

**What.** Nothing consumes the cross-org data in production:

1. `rate_snapshot.run(include_experimental_crossorg=False)` and
   `prequential.load_fight_table(with_crossorg=False)` both default off, and
   `refresh.py` never passes either flag.
2. The standard `2026-08-13` snapshot used to contain multiple names for
   different cross-org products. A bare `crossorg_fights.parquet` could mean
   different things depending on which producer ran last.

**Why B9 is its own blocker.** Anyone who flips `--experimental-crossorg` on the
standard snapshot merges **zero bouts** and gets an identical board. The obvious
inference — "cross-org makes no difference" — is wrong, and the run gives no
signal that it did nothing. On a UFC-only snapshot every `org_weight` is 1.0, so
the ablation is `0 (exactly), [0, 0]`: inert by construction, not by measurement.

**Resolution.** Make the no-op loud and the artifact choice explicit. Scopes now
resolve through `ratings/scope.py`; `majors` reads `majors_fights.parquet`,
`fightmatrix` reads `fightmatrix_crossorg_fights.parquet`, and a missing
requested scope raises instead of silently rating UFC-only.

## 4. B3 — scope truncation, and the Unified-Rules boundary *(RESOLVED; the decision is taken)*

**What.** Two different truncations get conflated. Keep them apart.

**(a) Non-UFC careers are absent.** PRIDE, WEC, Strikeforce, Affliction,
Bellator and RIZIN are ingested (`majors_bouts.parquet`, 6,199 bouts / 589
events) and unused. Measured effect of including them, core-only re-rank:
Wanderlei Silva from the zero-mass tie to 47 on 29 recovered bouts, José Aldo
33 → 11, Mauricio Rua 1,749 → 59, Urijah Faber 2,427 → 140. Blast radius: 24 in,
24 out of the top 100, Spearman 0.940, median top-50 move 14 places.

**(b) UFC 1–27 are excluded on purpose.** This is *not* missing data and *not* a
loader bug — an earlier read of mine said otherwise and was wrong. The 253 bouts
across 30 events (1994-03-11 → 2000-09-22) are scraped, parsed, and written to
`_excluded_bouts.csv` with `exclusion_reason = "pre_unified_rules"`.
`loaders/ufcstats_loader.py` states the rule:

> Unified-rules cutoff. UFC 28 (Tito Ortiz vs Yuki Kondo) was the first event
> under unified rules. Everything strictly before is dropped.

**The uncosted consequence.** That boundary is defensible on its own terms and
nobody costed what it does to the board. It means the engine structurally cannot
rank the 1993–2000 generation, and it is most of why Randy Couture scores zero:
his rated record is 20 bouts from 2000-11-17 against 30 in the FightMatrix
diagnostic cache, so roughly a third of his career — the early third — is
outside the window by design.

**The decision this forces (see §8).** Either the pre-unified era stays out and
the board says so in words — *"ranks the Unified-Rules era; fighters whose
careers predate it are not ranked"* — or the boundary applies to *presentation*
while the bouts still inform the rating. Both are defensible. Silently ranking
Couture 2,032nd is not.

## 5. B4 — the whole-career crawl truncates its own entrants *(CLOSED)*

**What.** The majors ingest is roster-complete *within six promotions*, which
applies the same censoring one level down: a Bellator fighter's non-Bellator
record is missing unless it happens to fall in another major.

**Evidence.** **22 of the joint top 60** hold under half their record inside the
UFC. `crossorg_careers_coverage.json`: the whole-career extension has read
**1,158 of 4,013** fighter pages (29%).

**Why it blocks.** Adopting the joint scope now means inheriting a smaller
version of the problem it fixes. That is a reason to finish the crawl, not a
reason to stay UFC-only.

**Resolution.** `build_crossorg_careers.py --from-cache` for the 2,855 pages
already cached, then crawl the remainder. Bounded: one page per fighter, no new
promotion to curate, no org weight to derive.

> **Done, 2026-08-24.** 4,501 of 4,501 fighter pages read. 63,813 bouts (6,199
> from event cards, 57,614 added by career pages), 28,491 distinct fighters,
> 1980-04-25 to 2026-10-03. Two things that were not expected:
>
> * **Identity resolution roughly tripled** — fighters shared with the UFC set
>   went 744 -> 2,160, and the `bout_evidence` tier fired for the first time (22
>   joins, plus 37 resolved collisions). It could not fire before: it joins an id
>   to a canonical fighter on shared (date, opponent) pairs, and a promotion's
>   event card carries no UFC bouts to share. A fighter page carries the
>   subject's whole record, so it does.
> * **The scope guard earned its place immediately** — 4,122 of the career-page
>   bouts are bouts already in `canonical_fights`, because a Sherdog fighter page
>   lists their UFC fights too. Without the guard every one of those would have
>   updated its participants' ratings twice.

## 6. B5 — the driftless prior deletes peaks

**What.** WHR's Wiener prior is driftless: `theta(t+D) − theta(t) ~ N(0, w²D)`.
It asserts a 24-year-old and a 41-year-old are equally likely to improve as to
decline, so the only way to explain "high, then very low" is to lower the whole
trajectory.

**Evidence.** Refitting with one fighter's post-peak suffix deleted, over 412
long careers: **375 (91%)** have that peak revised *down* by the full-career fit,
median **−73 points**, graded **+247 points per unit of post-cut win rate
(t = 18.3)**. **92 of 607** careers with ≥10 bouts are fitted as pure downward
ramps. The sign is wrong for a less-evidence explanation — less evidence pulls a
high rating toward the anchor, not away from it.

**Why it blocks.** Prime and Peak scores cannot be defended while a fighter's
peak is a function of how long they fought afterwards. It also blocks the age
work, which is the same parameter family.

**Resolution.** Plan §E6 / Phase 1b: age-dependent drift in the prior **mean**,
fitted prequentially. **Not** a decline penalty — the gradient already separates
Romero from Ferguson, and a penalty would post the same fact a third time,
breaking Single-Entry.

## 7. B6 — the board is not identified beyond first place *(RESOLVED by decision: tiers)*

**What.** Bootstrap rank intervals are wide enough that almost no rank
difference is claimable.

**Evidence.** Production 150-replicate bootstrap: median top-50 rank interval
width **157 places**. Jon Jones `[1, 2]` is the only tight interval in the top
20; the next narrowest is 17 wide. Natalia Silva `[19, 195]`, Merab Dvalishvili
`[5, 150]`. When the joint scope was tested, only **1 of 5** checked rank moves
had disjoint intervals.

**Why it blocks.** It blocks *validating* every other fix at the fighter level.
A change can move a point estimate 2,000 places and still be unresolved.

**Resolution.** Two candidate directions, and they are not exclusive:
report mass with its interval rather than rank; or revisit §E5 — the bar at 0.9
rests each career on few terms by design, and that is where the width comes from.
The docstring already states the trade honestly ("the price of measuring the
intended thing"). What is missing is a decision about whether the board should
publish a number it cannot defend.

## 8. B7 — the board prints a dense rank across a 2,366-way tie *(RESOLVED)*

**What.** Ranks **189 – 2,554** all hold a career mass of exactly zero — 2,366 of
2,554 fighters, one contiguous block, one distinct value of the secondary sort
key. `career_skill_mass` then breaks the tie on `repr(name)`.

**Consequence.** Forrest Griffin 945, Randy Couture 2,032, Wanderlei Silva 2,476
is **alphabetical ordering presented as a ranking**. Every one of those numbers
reads as a measurement and none of them is.

**Resolution.** Cheap and independent of everything else: rank with
`method="min"` so the tie prints as one place, or publish *"unranked — no year
above the bar"*. Presentation only; no mass changes.

## 9. B8 — the cross-era scale rests on 14 fighters

**What.** Comparing a 2000 rating with a 2024 one runs through a chain of
overlapping careers, and the chain has a bottleneck.

**Evidence.** The narrowest year-to-year link is **2000 → 2001, sharing 14
fighters**. Only **10 fighters** in the database were active both in 2000–2004
and in 2016–2026. Against that, conditional on record depth, a later debut is
worth **+5.74 rating points per year (t = 7.73)** — about +138 points across the
span, against a bar that rises only +73.

**Why it blocks.** Whether that gradient is the sport improving or the scale
drifting is **not identified** from this data, and no model choice resolves it.

**Resolution.** There is none by modelling. Publish the bridge width beside any
cross-era claim and treat the gradient as unattributed. The only real remedy is
more record per fighter — which is B3 and B4.

## 11. Decisions only you can make *(all taken, 2026-08-24)*

1. **Corpus.** Both, as separate named scopes, never silently merged.
2. **Unified-Rules boundary.** Admitted to the *rating*, carrying an explicit
   rules-era term. The term is estimated, not asserted: `build_rules_era_sweep.py`

The questions as originally posed follow.

### The questions as posed

1. **The Unified-Rules boundary (B3b).** Does UFC 1–27 stay out of the *rating*,
   or only out of the *presentation*? This decides whether Randy Couture is
   "unrankable, by a stated rule" or "rated on his whole career."
2. **What the board publishes when it cannot separate (B6).** A rank with a

## 12. Constraints that do not change

- **Single-Entry.** Every fact posted once. Opponent quality lives in the
  opponent's rating, achievement in the ledger, era in the reference field. Any
  fix that adds one of them to the score is wrong by construction.
- **No organisation weight.** Promotion strength is an output of the joint fit,
  never an input. A weight asserts the answer the fit exists to estimate.
- **Never tune to an external list.** FightMatrix and the UFC rankings are
  diagnostics to be explained, never targets.
- **Evidence discipline.** "Unresolved" when the interval crosses zero. A rank
  change is not a claim about a fighter without its interval beside it.
- **Re-bootstrap anything that changes the board.** A better point estimate with
  a wider interval has not obviously improved anything, and must say so.
